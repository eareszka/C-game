# Enemy sprite style

The one rule for every enemy battle sprite (the user, 2026-11-04, tightened
2026-11-05). The 26 sprites the user picked, on the style board, show the look.
Where a pick differs from a limit written here, the written limit wins.

The user names one pick as inspiration for other sprites: 34 Anaye, the armoured
figure on the board with the feather crest and purple axe. Take from how it is
drawn, not what it is.

## The style

NES style at every size. Sprites are crisp, chunky and readable, with flat
colours and a black outline, and every pixel looks placed on purpose. A big enemy
is big flat shapes and few lines, at the same 1 px pixels as the player. Plain
areas are welcome.

## Size

Enemies grow as their tier rises, and each tier starts just above the one before
it. Within a tier, a creature's size in its lore sets where it falls in the range,
and your brief gives the exact number. A creature that is very small or very large
in its lore can go up to 10% past its tier's range. The game draws every sprite at
2x. Never scale art: one art pixel stays one art pixel. Keep the frame snug to the
drawing, because the hit circle comes from the frame's size.

| Tier | Longer side |
|---|---|
| Starter | 20 px |
| Low | 21 to 31 px |
| Medium | 33 to 44 px |
| Upper | 45 to 56 px |
| Hard | 57 to 66 px |
| Severe | 67 to 80 px |
| Elite | 81 to 96 px |
| Boss 1 | 100 to 150 px |
| Boss 2 | 160 to 260 px |
| Boss 3 | 280 px |

## Shape

- **One creature, one strong silhouette.** It reads at a glance on black.
- **Exaggerate what makes it that creature.** Small sprites can be stubby and
  cute, and bigger ones can take on real proportions.
- **Clean runs and clear steps.** Avoid long smooth curves that look like vector
  art.
- **Every part is its own part.** Limbs, fins and tails are separate, and each can
  be traced from the body to its tip. Parts never merge into one shape.
- **Proportions make sense** for that creature. It looks like it could stand and
  move.
- **No motif is repeated identically.** Scales, plates and bricks differ in size,
  spacing and shape, and some are left out. A few well-placed marks beat a pattern
  covering the body. Mirroring a whole body is fine.

## Colour and shade

- Only colours from the game's palette, 4 to 5 plus black. Up to 8 only when your
  brief says the design needs it.
- Flat hard fills. No gradients, soft edges or see-through pixels. No dithering,
  hatching or speckle.
- Two flat tones per material: a base and one shadow. No highlights and no gradual
  shading.
- Light comes from the upper left, and shade follows the body.
- Tiny details are welcome when each one is a real feature, such as a tooth, a
  claw, a horn tip or a stripe.

## Line

- A black outline, 1 px wide, round the silhouette. Never doubled.
- Black lines inside separate the parts. Use few of them: a line is there because
  two parts meet, not to fill a surface. A small sprite is about 8 to 15% black
  inside the outline, and a big flat one has less.

## The black arena

The battle draws on pure black, so the player never sees a black outline.

- No part may be drawn only in black, because it vanishes.
- No body may be mostly close to black.
- The sprite must read on black. Black is what the player gets.

## Eyes

Each eye is one solid mark of 1 to 2 px: black on lit skin, or one accent colour
on dark hide. Never a ring, an iris with a pupil or a white glint. X eyes and skull
sockets are allowed as character features.

## Poses

When a brief asks for poses: 4 to 5 distinct poses, each held 260 ms, with big
steps between them. Every view and pose has the same body proportions. No water
under an enemy.

## Keeping the design

The user likes every enemy's design. Keep the creature in the brief: the same
signature features, main colours and mood. Change how it is drawn, not what it is.

## Done

A sprite is done when the checker shows no failures and the user has approved it.
