The art style, as it stands

The game has a real shared style at the level of materials, but not yet a shared way of drawing at large sizes.

  • What it is: folk-creature cartoons, with one creature, one joke, chunky hard-edged shapes and a face you read at once. Colours are from the game's palette, usually 8 plus black at most.
  • Where it works: Starter through Medium, repeated marks included. Upper is nearly there, with four anatomy misses.
  • Where it drifts: Hard and up. The redraws there show the same habits, the old sprites use softer shading, and the new families are cleaner again.
  • Bosses are the biggest gap. They jump in size and softness from Elite, and most are still old sprites at sizes off the table.

Why it breaks down

  • The first redraw rule. {{FIRST_RULE}} of the {{DRAWN_N}} remaining agent redraws came from the first rule, which pushed agents to fill big bodies with texture. {{LOOPS}} used loops in code, which is how identical bricks and scales get made.
  • The same habits repeat. The toothy mouth, the mirrored front pose, the row of toe ticks and the stamped fur stroke come back on different creatures, which is much of what reads as AI-made above Upper.
  • Nothing checks anatomy. Bodies that don't join up, like 28, 61, 62 and 77, pass every number.
  • The written rule is out of date. art/direction/SPRITE_STYLE.md and the checker still ask for things your picks break, like 4 to 5 colours and 9 to 14% inner black. They haven't been changed.
  • Size order is unsettled. {{N_OLD}} old sprites keep their old sizes, so 100 Flesh Planet is smaller than Boss 1's 30 Dajna, and 24 and 33 are tiny for their tiers.
